# Heart Failure Mortality Risk Analysis

## Overview

This project analyzes clinical and demographic factors associated with mortality among patients with heart failure. Using a dataset of 299 patient records and 13 clinical variables, the analysis explores relationships between patient characteristics and mortality outcomes through data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, statistical analysis, and visualization.

The goal is to identify meaningful patterns in the data and translate the findings into actionable insights related to mortality-risk reduction.

## Research Question

**Which patient characteristics are most strongly associated with mortality among patients with heart failure?**

## Dataset

The analysis uses the **Heart Failure Clinical Records Dataset**, containing 299 patient records and 13 clinical variables.

Key variables include:

* Age
* Sex
* Ejection fraction
* Serum creatinine
* Serum sodium
* Platelets
* Diabetes
* Smoking
* Anaemia
* High blood pressure
* Creatinine phosphokinase
* Follow-up period
* Mortality outcome

The dataset contains:

* **299 total patients**
* **13 clinical variables**


## Methodology

The analysis follows a standard data science workflow:

1. **Data Loading & Validation**

   * Imported the clinical dataset.
   * Examined data types, dimensions, missing values, and descriptive statistics.

2. **Data Cleaning & Preprocessing**

   * Validated patient-level records.
   * Prepared categorical and numerical variables for analysis.
   * Converted mortality outcomes into interpretable `Died` and `Survived` groups.

3. **Exploratory Data Analysis**

   * Compared clinical characteristics between mortality groups.
   * Examined distributions and relationships using statistical summaries and visualizations.

4. **Statistical Analysis**

   * Evaluated relationships between patient characteristics and mortality outcomes.
   * Used statistical comparisons to identify variables associated with differences between mortality groups.

5. **Interpretation**

   * Translated quantitative findings into healthcare-oriented insights.
   * Identified potential areas for further investigation and mortality-risk reduction.
